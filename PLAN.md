# Plan: Distribution Reps

The Reps framework, pointed at the live gap. The builds are verified —
Permit at 413 passed / 3 skipped, AgentReady reproducing at 47/100 — and
the record says the same thing twelve reports running: distribution is
what's behind, not building. So the reps are distribution reps.

Every rep ends in a receipt: a live URL or a Sent confirmation. Staged,
drafted, or ready does not count as a rep.

## Rep 1: One clean release

**Builds on**: The staged work already waiting — the Oct 28 story-post
pair (LinkedIn + X) is written and claims-checked, and pairs like it keep
being staged and expiring unposted.
**New challenge**: Release one pair verbatim, start to finish. No edits
mid-flight, no re-staging.
**Done when**: Both posts are live, URLs recorded.
**Expected difficulty**: Baseline — the material already exists; the only
muscle is letting it go.

## Rep 2: One new channel

**Builds on**: Rep 1's release.
**New challenge**: Add exactly one channel Rep 1 didn't use — send one
case-study package from the completed five-package kit to one named
recipient.
**Done when**: The package is in Sent, addressed to a person with a name,
not a list.
**Expected difficulty**: Harder (because you've mastered Rep 1) — this
one needs a recipient chosen, not just a post released.

## Rep 3: In-person, with follow-through

**Builds on**: Reps 1–2.
**New challenge**: Convert one in-person contact from the meetup circuit
into a sent follow-up within 24 hours of the conversation.
**Done when**: The follow-up is sent inside the 24-hour window, receipt
recorded.
**Expected difficulty**: Harder again — the clock is the new constraint,
and the material is written fresh for one person.

## The gate, stated once

Every rep's final step is a Tier-3 act — a public post or an outside
send — so every rep completes on Marcus Richards's own release word for
that specific act. The plan stages everything up to that word; the word
is part of the rep, not a delay in it.
